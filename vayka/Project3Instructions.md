Grading Criteria
The course project has a weight of 30% of your course final grade, with two sprints you will have to work on as a team: each costing 15% of your course final grade. The project and each of its sprints will be graded using the format below, based on two core factors: product and process.
Concerning your product, your team will need to satisfy their client (a member from the instructional team: i.e., your mentor TA) based on the user stories you all agreed to complete for the sprint. ==User stories will be graded as all-or-nothing==: partial functionality will not earn you points.
Concerning your process, your team will need to follow Scrum and its different elements. Communication, cooperation, planning, feedback, and balance are essential. Teams may be sanctioned for the following issues:
Lack of effective communication with the client.
Lack of effective communication among teammates.
Lack of planning and follow-ups (daily standups).
Unbalanced distribution of work.
Lack of cooperation and conflict resolution.
Per our syllabus, all communication among teams, clients, and members must be via our institutional channels: Microsoft Teams, Canvas, Ed Discussion, and GT Outlook.
All points will be assessed by your client TA, so it is the student's responsibility to frequently check in with their mentor TA. At the end of each sprint (15% of your course final grade) of the project, based on these criteria, your grade will be calculated as follows:
Outstanding (100% of the grade): Both product and process were successful! a. Product: Your app successfully works, features all user stories required, and presents a usable and coherent graphical user interface (GUI) that aligns with the stated design expectations (see Design Guidelines section). AND b. Process: The process was successful and incorporated effective communication, cooperation, planning, feedback, and balance. If issues happened, they were reported proactively and found resolution before the end of the sprint. Additionally, all process deliverables (sprint planning, review, and retrospective) were successfully done and submitted on time.

Strike System
The strike system is a way for the instructional team to ensure contribution fairness and individual accountability. Individuals who have received a strike may have the strike lifted.
Our strike system entails:
Strike 1: There is a written warning via email from the client TA, CCing your instructor and head TA. This first warning does not lead to a grade penalty.
Strike 2: There is a written warning via email from the client TA, CCing your instructor and head TA. This second warning leads to a grade deduction of 30 points (out of 100 points) on the project's sprint. We then apply what's described in Disclaimer #4.
Strike 3: There is a written warning via email from the client TA, CCing your instructor and head TA. This third and final warning leads to a grade deduction of 100 points (out of 100 points) on the project's sprint.
What behavior will warrant a strike? A strike may be issued for, but is not limited to, the following behaviors:
Repeated failure to complete assigned user stories or sprint responsibilities
Missing or consistently disengaging from team meetings, standups, or sprint reviews
Lack of meaningful contribution to the project's product or process
Poor or unprofessional communication with teammates or the client TA
Ignoring feedback or action items provided by the instructional team
Claiming work that was not completed or was completed by another team member
Repeatedly submitting low-effort or non-functional work that hinders team progress
Strikes are not issued for honest mistakes, learning struggles, or temporary difficulties when proactively and professionally communicated to the mentor TA.
Can I get a strike lifted? Yes. A strike may be lifted at the discretion of the instructional team if the student:

Demonstrates clear improvement in contribution and engagement

Design Guidelines
Ultimately, you will create a user-facing web application for a client; for this to be successful, your application must adhere to the following requirements:
Client TA preferences. You are designing for a client. Design decisions (layout, navigation, feature placement, and overall experience) should align with feedback and expectations communicated by your mentor TA.
Coherent brand. Across your website, you should demonstrate a clear and consistent brand identity. Users should feel they are interacting with a single, cohesive product—not a collection of disconnected pages. This includes:
Consistent color palette
Consistent typography
Reusable UI components (buttons, cards, headers, etc.)
Consistent tone and visual language across pages
Theme alignment. Your visual design should clearly reflect your chosen domain and End User persona (e.g., educational, professional, playful, analytical). Design choices should support the purpose of the chatbot and the needs of its users, not distract them.

During the demo, the client TA may ask students to explain aspects of their project, including (but not limited to):

Individual contributions and understanding
Each sprint demo will assess five competency areas. For each competency area that is not satisfactorily demonstrated, up to 3 points may be deducted from the team's sprint grade.

Context Description
RAG-based systems have become exceedingly popular in industry for building internal chatbot solutions for companies, enabling users to interact with a large amount of proprietary knowledge easily through natural language queries.
Your team will design and build a RAG-based chatbot that users can interact with through a hosted web application. The chatbot should retrieve information from a curated dataset and generate responses grounded in that data.
The system must support user interaction, backend data ingestion and retrieval, and administrative controls. Teams are encouraged to be creative in selecting their chatbot's domain (e.g., sports analytics, language learning, games, educational tutors), provided all functional requirements are met and approved by the team's client TA.

As an End User, I want to ask natural language questions about the selected topic, so I can receive relevant information.
As an End User, I want responses to be grounded in the provided dataset, so I can trust their accuracy.
As an End User, I want to see which documents or sources were used to generate an answer, so I can verify the response.
As a Developer, I want to define and update the dataset used by the RAG system, so the chatbot's knowledge stays current.
As a Developer, I want to ingest data using at least two distinct ingestion methods (e.g., API-based ingestion, document scraping, or direct text/file uploads) so I can build a rich and flexible knowledge base.
As a Developer, I want to clean and preprocess data before ingestion, so retrieval results are accurate and relevant.
As a Developer, I want the system to dynamically select or construct prompts based on the type of user question, so I can control the chatbot's behavior for different intents (e.g., factual lookup vs. explanation vs. creative response). a. Prompt behavior must be determined programmatically at runtime; manually editing a single static prompt string is not sufficient.
As a Developer, I want to test response accuracy against known questions, so I can validate system correctness.
Note: For the administrator user stories, these must be functionalities on the website itself, not only the cloud console.
As an Administrator, I want to monitor system performance (latency, failures), so the chatbot meets responsiveness requirements.
As an Administrator, I want to manually clear or reload the chatbot's knowledge base so I can recover from incorrect or outdated data.
As an Administrator, I want to view basic usage logs (e.g., number of queries, timestamps, response times) so I can understand how the chatbot is being used.
